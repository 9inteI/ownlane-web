Every so often the idea comes back that you should run breathing only through your nose: that it filters, that it humidifies, that it improves performance. The first two are true. The third doesn't hold up.

## What has been measured

When nasal, oral and combined breathing are compared in exercise testing, the conclusion repeats: no meaningful performance differences. And breathing only through the nose limits ventilation as soon as intensity rises, because the nose simply moves less air.

There's an interesting detail: in some studies perceived effort was higher breathing through the mouth without any change in power. So the nose can make you feel more comfortable without making you more capable.

<Callout>Nose breathing isn't bad. It's fine and it does nothing. The problem is selling it as a performance boost.</Callout>

## Why the mouth opens on its own

Because the body opens the mouth when it needs to move more air, and that always happens as soon as you go beyond a very gentle jog. It isn't a technique fault: it's the correct response. Forcing yourself not to is imposing an artificial limit.

## One use that does make sense

If you force yourself to breathe through your nose on easy days, it forces you to actually go easy: as soon as you speed up the air runs out and you slow down by yourself. As a voluntary brake it works, just like the talk test. But what's helping you there is the low pace, not the nose.

## What to do tomorrow

- Breathe however it comes. If your mouth opens, you're doing it right.
- If you want a way not to overdo easy days, use the talk test, which has the advantage of being validated.
- In cold weather or with allergies, breathing through the nose for a while does help the air arrive less dry. That's comfort, not performance.
